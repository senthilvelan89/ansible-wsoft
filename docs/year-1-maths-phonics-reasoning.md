# Year 1 at home: maths, phonics and reasoning

A from-the-start plan for a **Grade 1 / Year 1** child (about 6–7 years old) in Victoria. Written as a companion to [williams-landing-schools-entry-guide.md](williams-landing-schools-entry-guide.md).

**Do not start selective-exam coaching in Year 1.** Suzanne Cory, EduTest, HAP and NAPLAN are years away. What actually predicts later strength is: accurate decoding, number sense to 20, and the habit of thinking out loud. Cramming practice papers now burns kids out and does not teach those things.

This is a home plan, not a diagnosis or a school program. If reading or number is far behind after a term of daily practice, ask the school and consider a SPELD Vic or educational-psych assessment.

---

## How to start this week

1. Ask the Year 1 teacher: **which phonics program** they use (Phonics Plus, Little Learners Love Literacy, InitiaLit, Sounds-Write, Sound Waves, Jolly Phonics, or something else) and **which decodable level** your child is on.
2. Match home reading to that sequence. Do not mix random “levelled” readers that ask the child to guess from the picture.
3. Buy or print the **starter kit** below. You do not need a tutoring centre yet.
4. Run the **daily 40-minute rhythm** for four weeks before you add anything else.

If the long-term goal is **Suzanne Cory / EduTest / HAP**, use the weekday timetable in [grade-1-competitive-exam-pathway.md](grade-1-competitive-exam-pathway.md). Keep **Reading Eggs as a 10-minute reward**, not the whole lesson.

If you do not know the school program yet, start with **SPELD SA free decodables** plus a ten-frame and counters. That combination is enough for month one.

---

## Daily rhythm (keep it short)

Year 1 attention is short. **Little and every day beats a Sunday worksheet pile.**

| Block | Time | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Phonics / reading** | 15–20 min | Ear games (2–3 min) → review known sounds → blend a few new words → read a **decodable** book → 4–6 words to spell / write |
| **Maths** | 15–20 min | Hands-on first (ten-frame, counters, Numicon or Bond Blocks) → say the idea in words → then 4–8 written items, not a booklet |
| **Reasoning** | 10–15 min, 4 days a week | A game, puzzle or “what’s the same / what’s different?” talk. Not a test paper. |
| **Read-aloud** | 15–20 min at bedtime | **You** read a real picture book or chapter book above their reading level. This builds vocabulary and comprehension while they are still decoding slowly. |

Total sit-down teaching: about **40 minutes**. Bedtime story is extra and should feel like a treat.

**Rules that make this work**

- Same time most days (after snack, before screens).
- Stop while they are still succeeding. One more page of tears undoes the week.
- You model; they try; you correct kindly and immediately. Do not let them guess a word from the picture.
- Praise the **strategy** (“you blended /c/ /a/ /t/”) not “you’re so smart”.

## Phonics — train from the start

Victoria’s government schools now teach **systematic synthetic phonics** in Prep–Year 2 (at least **25 minutes a day**). There is a **Year 1 Phonics Check** (mandatory in government primaries from 2026). Home should **echo the school**, not fight it.

### The skill order (do not skip)

1. **Phonemic awareness (ears only, no letters).** Can they hear that *cat* is /c/ /a/ /t/? Blend, segment, then swap a sound (*cat* → *hat*). This is the engine of reading.
2. **Grapheme–phoneme map.** One sound, one (or two) letters, in a fixed sequence. Typical Year 1 path: secure all single letters → consonant blends (*st, bl, cr*) → digraphs (*sh, ch, th, ck, ng, qu*) → long-vowel teams and split digraphs (*ai, ee, igh, oa, a_e*).
3. **Blend to read, segment to spell.** Same code, both directions, every day.
4. **Decodable text.** The book only uses sounds they have been taught, plus a tiny set of heart/tricky words (*the, I, said*).
5. **Fluency.** Reread yesterday’s book until it is smooth, then a new one.
6. **Handwriting + dictation.** Write the sounds they can read. Pencil grip and letter formation matter; messy letters slow spelling later.

**Heart words:** teach the tricky part only (*said* = “s” “ai that looks weird” “d”). Do not flashcard every regular word as a “sight word”.

### What a 15-minute phonics session looks like

1. **Ear game (2 min).** “I say /sh/ /i/ /p/ — what’s the word?” Then “What’s the first sound in *ship*?” Then “Say *ship* without /sh/.”
2. **Quick review (3 min).** Flash 8–10 known letter–sound cards. Mix in 2 they still mix up (*b/d*, *m/n*).
3. **New or sticky code (3 min).** One grapheme. Three example words. They trace the letters in the air, then write them.
4. **Read (5–7 min).** One short decodable. Child points under each word. If they stall, you say the sounds with them, then they blend. Never “look at the picture and guess”.
5. **Write (2 min).** You say 4 words from the book. They segment and write. Tick each sound, not just the whole word.

### Good phonics materials

**Free / cheap (start here)**

| Material | Why | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **SPELD SA Phonic Books** | Hundreds of free decodable PDFs, in Jolly Phonics and Sounds-Write sequences, plus worksheets | [speldsa.org.au](https://speldsa.org.au/pages/speld-sa-phonic-books) |
| School take-home decodables | Match the exact code being taught | Ask the teacher for the current set / app login |
| **Heggerty-style ear games** | 2–3 minutes of oral blending and segmenting | Search “Heggerty parent newsletter Foundation / Year 1”; or invent your own with fridge-word games |
| Letter tiles or fridge magnets | Physical blending (*c-a-t* push together) | Any cheap alphabet set. Avoid “A is for Apple” picture clutter during blending. |
| **Year 1 Phonics Check** practice (later in the year) | Shows if blending of real and nonsense words is secure | Teacher will run the real check. Do not drill it all year. |

**Buy if you want a proper home shelf**

| Material | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Little Learners Love Literacy** Pip and Tim / Wiz Kids decodables | School uses LLLL, or you want a clean Australian sequence with Milo. |
| **Phonics Books UK – Dandelion Readers** (Units 1–20, then Extended Code) | Excellent if school uses Sounds-Write, or as a second set. Sold via DSF / SPELD shops. |
| **InitiaLit Readers** Levels 10–16 | Only if the school is on InitiaLit–1. Do not mix sequences blindly. |
| **Sound Waves Decodable Readers** | Only if the school is on Sound Waves. |
| **Jolly Phonics** student books + decodables | Fine home program if the school uses Jolly or SPELD SA JP books. |
| **Teach Your Monster to Read** (app) | Short, systematic, good as a 10-minute reward, not the whole lesson. |
| **Phonics Hero** | Australian, follows a clear code. Use the teacher’s recommended stage. |

**Skip or use carefully**

- **PM / Fountas & Pinnell style levelled readers** as the *main* home book — they teach guessing.
- **Reading Eggs / similar** as the only program — mixed quality; OK as extra games if the core is decodable reading with you.
- Giant **sight-word lists** of decodable words (*and, went, jump*).
- Starting **chapter-book “pressure”** before they can decode a Year 1 decodable smoothly.

### If they are stuck

- Still guessing from pictures → take the pictures away (cover them) and go back a decodable level.
- Knows letter names but not sounds → drop names for a month; sounds only.
- Can’t hear the middle vowel → more ear games, fewer books, for two weeks.
- After a full term of daily practice they are still well behind classmates → talk to the teacher; ask about MiniLit / MacqLit / school intervention; SPELD Vic can advise.

## Maths — train from the start

Victorian schools aim for about **60 minutes of maths a day**. Home does not need to copy that. Home should make **number feel obvious**.

A **Year 1 Numeracy Check** is being piloted (mandatory from 2027 in government primaries). The target is deep Year 1 knowledge, not Year 3 worksheets.

### The skill order (do not skip)

1. **Subitising.** See 4 dots and know it is 4 without counting one-by-one.
2. **Count well.** Forwards and backwards, from any number, to 20 then 100. One-to-one when counting objects.
3. **Number bonds to 10, then 20.** Instant: 7 + ? = 10, 8 + 5 by “make 10 then 3 more”. This is the Year 1 superpower.
4. **Teen numbers as 10 and some ones.** 14 is a full ten-frame plus 4, not a “1” and a “4” that live apart.
5. **Add and subtract within 20** with strategies (count on, doubles, near-doubles, make-ten), not finger-counting forever.
6. **Place value to 100.** Tens and ones, locate on a number line, compare.
7. **Then** skip-count 2s, 5s, 10s; easy money; simple length/time; sharing into equal groups (start of multiplication).

Reasoning in maths at this age is: “How do you know?” and “Can you do it another way?” — not algebra.

### What a 15-minute maths session looks like

1. **Warm number talk (3 min).** Show 8 counters or a Numicon 8. “How do you see it? 5 and 3? Double 4? 10 take 2?”
2. **Hands-on (7 min).** One idea only. Example: build every way to make 10 on a ten-frame. Or split 14 into 10 and 4, then 14 − 4, 14 − 10.
3. **Say it (2 min).** Child explains to you in a full sentence: “Fourteen is one ten and four ones.”
4. **Tiny written bit (3 min).** 4–8 items of the *same* idea. Stop. No mixed worksheet of 40 questions.

Living maths counts: cooking (½ cup), setting the table (“we need 2 more”), card games, shop play with 10c and 20c.

### Good maths materials

**Free / cheap (start here)**

| Material | Why |
|---|---|
| **Two ten-frames + 20 counters** (buttons, pasta, glass gems) | The single most useful Year 1 tool. Print frames from any “ten frame PDF”. |
| **Hundred chart** and a **0–20 number line** | Counting on, +10, locating numbers. |
| **Playing cards** (face cards removed) | Make 10, war (compare), doubles. |
| **Dice** (one 6-face, later a 10-face) | Addition races, “one more / one less”. |
| School **Victorian Lesson Plans / workbook** pages the teacher sends | Aligns with what they just learned. |

**Worth buying**

| Material | Why |
|---|---|
| **Numicon At Home** (Oxford, ages 5–7) | Shapes that make number bonds visible. Feely-bag games build mental images. |
| **Rekenrek** (20-bead abacus) | Fast subitising and “5 and a bit”. |
| **Bond Blocks** (Australian, maps to Vic curriculum) | Best if the school already uses them; otherwise Numicon + ten-frames is enough at home. Classroom kits are expensive — don’t buy a school kit for one child. |
| **Cuisenaire rods** | Length and “how numbers hide inside numbers”. |
| **Targeting Maths Year 1** (Australian) or a **Singapore Primary Mathematics 1A/1B** home set | One structured book for when the concrete idea is already solid. Do not start with the book. |
| **Mathletics / Mathseeds** | Practice after teaching, 10 minutes max. Not the teacher. |

**Skip**

- Racing through **Year 2/3 workbooks** because they can “do sums”.
- **Kumon-style** speed sheets before bonds to 10 are instant.
- Teaching the standard written algorithm for 27 + 35 in Year 1. They need tens-and-ones first.

### “Strong” in Year 1 maths looks like

- Bonds to 10 in a few seconds, most of the time.
- Explains 13 as 10 + 3 with materials, then on paper.
- Adds 8 + 6 by making 10, not by counting from 1.
- Comfortable being wrong and trying another way.
- Does **not** need to be doing long multiplication.

## Reasoning — train from the start

Reasoning at 6 is **not** a verbal-reasoning exam. It is: notice a pattern, hold two ideas, explain why, change your mind.

Do this with **games and talk**. Save ACER / EduTest papers for Year 5–8 (see the schools guide).

### The skill order

1. **Sort and classify.** Buttons by colour, then by colour *and* size. “Why does this one not belong?”
2. **Pattern.** Copy, continue, then invent AB, AAB, ABC patterns with blocks, claps, or drawings.
3. **Same / different / analogy.** “Sock is to foot as glove is to …?” Start with real objects, then pictures.
4. **Spatial.** Rotate a shape, copy a tangram, give directions (“two steps forward, turn”).
5. **Simple logic.** “If all the red cars are in the box, can a red car be on the table?”
6. **Explain.** Every answer gets a “how do you know?”

### What a 10-minute reasoning session looks like

Pick **one**:

- Finish a 24–48 piece jigsaw (later 60–80).
- Copy a pattern-block picture, then make a new one with the same number of shapes.
- Tangram animal from a card.
- “Odd one out” with four fridge magnets — they must give a reason, then you give a different reason for a different item.
- After the bedtime story: “Why did she do that? What might happen next? What would you do?”

### Good reasoning materials

**Games (this is the real curriculum)**

| Game | Skills |
|---|---|
| **Rush Hour Junior** | Planning, working backwards |
| **Robot Turtles** or simple grid “program a toy” | Sequencing, if/then |
| **Blokus Junior** or **Blokus** | Spatial, blocking |
| **SET Junior** / **Uno** / **Sleeping Queens** | Attributes, patterns |
| **Connect 4**, **Guess Who**, **Memory** | Logic, working memory |
| **Snakes and Ladders** with two dice they add | Number + turn-taking |
| **Chess** (from late Year 1 if they ask) | Planning. 10 minutes. No rating chase. |
| **Katamino** / **SmartGames** one-player puzzles | Persistence, spatial |

**Physical kit**

- Pattern blocks + cards
- Tangram set
- Wooden cubes or Lego for “build what I built” (you hide yours)
- Jigsaws that slowly get harder

**Books / talk**

- You read: *The Gruffalo*, *Rosie Revere Engineer*, *Ada Twist*, *Anno’s Counting Book*, any Julia Donaldson, later early chapter books (*Fox and Fine Feathers*, *Zac Power*, *Maverick* — whatever they love).
- Ask open questions. Wait 5 seconds. Don’t jump in.

**Skip**

- Year 1 “IQ test” books and “scholarship prep Grade 1” PDFs.
- Timed multiple-choice reasoning papers.
- Comparing them to a cousin who already does Kumon Level C.

## Four-term map (Year 1)

Use this as a default if the teacher has not given you a sequence. Stay on a step until it is easy on **three different days**.

### Term 1 — foundations

- **Phonics:** All single-letter sounds rock-solid. Blend 3-sound words (*sat, pin, frog* if blends are in). Daily decodable at that level. Ear games every day.
- **Maths:** Subitise to 6. Bonds to 5, then to 10 with a ten-frame. Count to 20 and back. Compare groups: more / less / same.
- **Reasoning:** Sort washing. Copy clap patterns. 12–24 piece puzzles.

### Term 2 — stretch the code and the ten

- **Phonics:** Consonant blends and first digraphs (*sh, ch, th, ck*). Reread for fluency. Short dictation sentences.
- **Maths:** All bonds to 10 instant. Teens as 10 + n. Add/subtract within 10 without counting from one. Number line 0–20.
- **Reasoning:** Odd-one-out with a reason. Rush Hour Junior. “What happens next?” after stories.

### Term 3 — fluency

- **Phonics:** More vowel teams as the school introduces them. Child reads a new decodable almost independently. You still do the bedtime *real* book.
- **Maths:** Add/subtract within 20 with make-ten. Place value to 50. Skip-count 2s and 10s. Doubles to 10 + 10.
- **Reasoning:** Tangrams. Two-attribute sort. Simple “if… then…” talk. Memory and Guess Who.

### Term 4 — lock it in

- **Phonics:** Should be ready for the **Phonics Check** style of reading (real and nonsense words, no picture clues). Keep decodables; add a *little* richer text only if decoding is automatic.
- **Maths:** Place value to 100. Locate numbers. 2s, 5s, 10s. Fair share (12 grapes, 3 plates). Time to the half hour if they are ready.
- **Reasoning:** Longer puzzles. Child teaches *you* a game. “How else could we solve this?”

School holidays: keep **10 minutes of decodable reading + one game**. Do not add a new program every break.

**Week 1 (under ~$40 if you print the books)**

- SPELD SA decodables (free PDF) or the school’s current set
- Printed ten-frames, 20 counters, a pack of cards, two dice
- Alphabet letter tiles or magnets

**You do not need**

- Selective / NAPLAN / ICAS prep
- Three different apps
- A desk full of workbooks they will not finish

## Ask the school these five questions

1. Which **phonics program and stage** is my child on? Which decodables should come home?
2. How did they go on any **phonics screen** (and, later, the Year 1 Phonics Check)?
3. In maths, are they still **counting from one**, or using **bonds and ten-frames**?
4. Is there a **home reading** expectation, and should I still read *to* them every night? (Yes.)
5. If they need extra help, what does the school run (MiniLit, MacqLit, maths intervention) before we pay a tutor?

Williams Landing / Point Cook / Hoppers Crossing families: Wyndham libraries often stock decodable series — ask for “decodable” or “phonic readers”, not just “Level 14”. SPELD Vic can point you to assessments if school support is not enough.

## When a tutor *does* make sense

Not in week one of Year 1.

Consider a short, specialist block if, after a term of the daily rhythm:

- decoding is still painfully slow or they refuse to blend, or
- they cannot hold number bonds to 10, or
- the teacher flags a gap and the school waitlist is long.

Then choose a tutor who uses **structured literacy** (synthetic phonics) and **concrete maths**, not a high-school student with a worksheet pack. The centres listed in the schools guide (Integral, Brainworks, Schoolola) are built for **later** selective / scholarship exams. For Year 1, a SPELD-aware literacy tutor or a primary teacher who knows ten-frames will usually be a better fit.

Put the exam pathway on the calendar much later: gentle enrichment through Year 3–4, structured reasoning/maths extension from Year 5, SEHS / EduTest prep from Year 6–8.
