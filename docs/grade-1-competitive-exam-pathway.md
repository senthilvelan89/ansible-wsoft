# Grade 1 daily plan toward later competitive exams

For a **Year 1 / Grade 1** child who may one day sit **Suzanne Cory (SEHS)**, **EduTest** (Westbourne, Werribee SELP, Alamanda Everest) or a school **HAP**. Companion to [year-1-maths-phonics-reasoning.md](year-1-maths-phonics-reasoning.md) and [williams-landing-schools-entry-guide.md](williams-landing-schools-entry-guide.md).

Those exams are in **Year 6–8**. They test **verbal reasoning** and **maths / numerical reasoning** (plus writing). They do **not** test who did the most practice papers in Grade 1.

**Year 1 job:** make reading accurate, numbers obvious, and “how do you know?” a habit. Reading Eggs can stay. It cannot be the whole plan.

---

## What those exams actually reward (so you train the right thing)

| Later exam piece | What it really is | What Grade 1 should build |
|---|---|---|
| **Verbal reasoning** | Vocabulary, analogies, same/different, following a written rule, reading a short text and thinking | Decoding so they can read; a huge listening vocabulary; sorting and “why”; talking about stories |
| **Reading comprehension** | Understand, infer, pick the best evidence | You reading rich books *to* them; then their own decodable reading |
| **Numerical / maths reasoning** | Number relationships, patterns, a new problem that is not a drilled sum | Bonds to 10, seeing 14 as 10+4, “another way?”, spatial puzzles |
| **Mathematics (EduTest)** | Year-level maths, done accurately and reasonably fast | Number sense first; speed comes later from knowing facts, not from timed sheets |
| **Writing (SEHS / EduTest)** | A short, organised piece under time | Daily oral sentences, then 1–2 written sentences from dictation. Not essays. |

If you only do Reading Eggs, you mostly get **screen practice and sight-word games**. That does not build analogies, number bonds, or writing.

## Reading Eggs week (one focus a day)

Use this if the app is the spine of the week. **One program per day.** Timer **15 minutes** in the app, then **5–10 minutes with you**. Total ~20–25 minutes, plus a bedtime story.

Login → **Start Learning** → tap **only** the program named for that day. Do not let her roam the games island for the whole session.

If you **do not have Mathseeds**, do Tuesday and Thursday with a ten-frame and counters instead of the app.

| Day | App (15 min) | Focus | After the app — with you (5–10 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | **Fast Phonics** → **Peaks** | **Sounds.** Letter–sound maps, blending. Stay on the current Peak; finish its activities, don’t skip ahead. | Say 4 sounds from today’s Peak. She writes the letters. Blend 3 words on letter tiles (*s-a-t*). |
| **Tuesday** | **Mathseeds** → **Lessons** | **Number.** Counting, ten-frames, “how many.” One lesson only. | Show the same number with counters. “How do you see it? Make 10.” |
| **Wednesday** | **Reading Eggs** → **Lessons** (the map) | **Words.** Blend to read the lesson book. Do the end-of-lesson book, not only the games. | She re-reads that book (or a SPELD SA / school decodable) **out loud** to you, finger under each word. |
| **Thursday** | **Mathseeds** → **Lessons** (next lesson) | **Adding / taking away.** Number lines, “how many left.” | 4 spoken problems: “You had 10, I ate 3. How many? How do you know?” No counting from 1 if she can use 10. |
| **Friday** | **Library** (or the lesson **book quiz**) | **Meaning.** Pick one short book at her level. Read it. Do the quiz. | 2 questions: “What happened? Why did they…?” This is verbal reasoning. |
| **Saturday** | **Reading Eggs** → **Spelling** (or Driving Tests if spelling is not on the account) | **Write the sounds.** One list / one test. | You dictate 4 words from the week. She writes. Tick each *sound*, not just the whole word. |
| **Sunday** | **No study.** App stays closed. | **Day off.** | Nothing on the plan. A normal bedtime story is fine if you already do one — it is not homework. |

**Every night Monday–Saturday:** you still read *to* her. That is the vocabulary engine for later verbal reasoning. **Sunday is no study** — skip the app and the “lesson” questions.

### What “done” looks like each day

- **Monday:** she can say the new sound without the picture clue.
- **Tuesday:** she can show today’s number on a ten-frame without counting every dot.
- **Wednesday:** she can read the lesson book to you, blending stuck words (no guessing from the picture).
- **Thursday:** she can explain one add/take-away with “I know because…”
- **Friday:** she can retell the library book in two sentences.
- **Saturday:** she can write 4 words using sounds, not copying.
- **Sunday:** no checklist. Day off.

### Rules so the week works

- Same time each weekday (after snack is fine).
- If she finishes the 15-minute lesson early, **stop**. Do not open a second program.
- If she is stuck on a Fast Phonics Peak for more than a week, stay there. Do not jump Peaks to earn eggs.
- **Mathseeds placement test** once, then follow the lessons in order.
- Friday Library: choose books she can decode, not the hardest cover.

## Reading Eggs — if you also run the longer 50-minute plan

The **Reading Eggs week** above is enough for Grade 1 most days. If you later add the longer timetable, Eggs stays the **last 10 minutes**, never the only thing.

**Do**

- Cap it at **10 minutes**, timer on.
- Prefer **Fast Phonics / map lessons** that match sounds the school has taught.
- Treat eggs and levels as a game score, not as “she can read.”
- If the subscription includes **Mathseeds**, same rule: after ten-frame maths, not instead of it.

**Don’t**

- Let it replace a **decodable book with you** (finger under the word, blend out loud).
- Let her guess from pictures or skip words to win the animation.
- Stack a second literacy app on top.
- Sit her on it for 30–40 minutes “because she likes it.”

If she fights the 15-minute phonics-with-you block but begs for Reading Eggs, eggs come **only after** the book. That is the deal.

## The daily timetable (print this)

**Aim: 5 weekdays. About 50 minutes after school, plus a bedtime story.** Weekends are lighter.

Suggested slot: **after snack, before screens** (for example 4:00–4:50). If afternoons are chaos, do **7:15–7:50** in the morning and keep only the bedtime story at night.

### Monday–Thursday

| Time | Block | Maps to | You actually do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Phonics with you** | Later verbal (must be able to read) | Ear game 2 min → 8 sound cards → one short **decodable** → 4 words to write |
| 0:15–0:30 | **Maths with you** | Later numerical reasoning | Ten-frame / counters. One idea. “How do you know?” Then 4–8 written items |
| 0:30–0:40 | **Reasoning play** | Verbal *or* maths reasoning (see weekly rotation) | Game, puzzle, or talk. No worksheet pack. |
| 0:40–0:50 | **Reading Eggs** | Extra decoding practice / reward | Timer 10 min. Then device away. |
| Night 15–20 min | **You read to her** | **Biggest verbal-reasoning builder** | Picture book or early chapter book **above** her reading level. Ask 2 questions. |

### Friday

- 10 min decodable reread (fluency)
- 15 min family game (Uno, Guess Who, Connect 4, Rush Hour Junior)
- Reading Eggs optional
- Bedtime story as usual

### Weekend

- **Saturday:** one longer puzzle (jigsaw, tangram, Rush Hour) **or** a board game with you. No “catch-up workbook.”
- **Sunday:** **no study.** No app, no phonics, no maths, no quizzes.

**Hard cap:** if she is tired or teary on a weekday, stop after phonics + story. Do not make it up on Sunday.

## Weekly reasoning rotation (this is the competitive-exam seed)

Alternate so both engines grow. Same 10-minute slot.

| Day | Focus | Grade 1 activity (examples) | Later exam echo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Verbal | Four fridge magnets: “Which does not belong? Why? Can you make a *different* one the odd one out?” | Classification, multiple valid rules |
| **Tue** | Maths | Number talk: show 8. “How do you see it?” Then “make 10 three ways” on a ten-frame | Quantitative reasoning, number structure |
| **Wed** | Verbal | After 5 pages of the bedtime book (or a short read-aloud now): “Why did she do that? What might happen next? What would you do?” | Inference, comprehension |
| **Thu** | Maths / spatial | Rush Hour Junior, tangram, or “build what I built” with cubes (your model hidden) | Non-verbal / spatial reasoning |
| **Fri** | Mix | Board game. Make her explain a rule in her own words | Following rules, working memory |

Once a week, add **one spoken analogy** (objects first, not papers):

- “Sock is to foot as glove is to …?”
- “Bird is to nest as bee is to …?”
- “Knife is to cut as pencil is to …?”

If she cannot read the words yet, **you say them**. Verbal reasoning starts in the ears.

## What to say (scripts)

**Verbal**

- “Give me a reason. Now give me a *different* reason.”
- “Say that sentence another way.”
- “What is the same about these two? What is different?”
- “Which word does not fit: *cat, dog, banana, fish*? Why?”

**Maths**

- “Don’t count from one. What do you already know?”
- “Is there another way?”
- “Fourteen is one ten and how many ones? Show me.”
- “I hid some. There were 10, now I see 6. How many are hiding? How do you know?”

**Praise the process:** “You blended” / “You tried a second way.” Not “you’re a genius.” Competitive kids who only hear “smart” freeze when Year 8 papers get hard.

## Materials next to Reading Eggs

You already have the app. Add these so the 40 minutes with you have something to hold.

| Must have | Why |
|---|---|
| School or **SPELD SA** decodable (paper) | Real blending. App is not enough. |
| Two **ten-frames** + 20 counters | Maths reasoning starts here. |
| Letter tiles / magnets | Push *c-a-t* together. |
| Pack of cards, two dice | Make-10, compare, doubles. |

| Add when the habit sticks | Why |
|---|---|
| **Rush Hour Junior** | Planning, working backwards (quantitative / spatial). |
| Pattern blocks or tangram | Spatial. |
| Numicon or rekenrek | See number bonds. |
| Library card | Endless read-alouds. This is the verbal curriculum. |

Do **not** buy Year 4–6 selective / OC / EduTest papers in Grade 1.

## Year-by-year ladder (so Grade 1 has a destination)

Keep this on the fridge. Change the daily plan only when the current year is easy.

| Year | Daily core | Reasoning | Exams / tutoring |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | This timetable. Decodables + ten-frames + read-alouds. Reading Eggs as reward. | Games, odd-one-out, number talks, puzzles | **None.** No NAPLAN coaching (NAPLAN starts Year 3 and is not the selective exam). |
| **3** | Independent reading 15 min (real books, not only the app). Maths: bonds to 20 instant, place value, times-2/5/10. | Analogies with words they can read. Chess optional. | Optional one fun competition (e.g. a school maths challenge). Not ICAS as a lifestyle. |
| **4** | Read 20 min daily. Written explanations: “I know because…” | Longer puzzles, simple logic grid (age-appropriate). | Still enrichment, not papers. |
| **5** | Strong classroom maths + wide reading. | **1–2 untimed** EduTest-style questions a week if she enjoys them. | Light look at what Westbourne / HAP tests *look* like. No weekly coaching centre yet unless she is bored at school. |
| **6** | If Westbourne / scholarship is a goal: EduTest familiarisation. | Mixed verbal + numerical practice, still untimed first | Tutoring optional. HAP / Everest if at a Point Cook P–9. |
| **7** | School maths must be solid (Year 8 content is the SEHS ceiling). | Weekly reasoning set, then review mistakes | Brainworks / Integral become useful *here*, not in Year 1. |
| **8** | SEHS year. Official **ACER** practice tests. | Timed papers closer to June | Sit the exam once. See the schools guide. |

NAPLAN in Years 3, 5, 7, 9 is a snapshot. It is **not** the Cory exam. Do not rebuild the whole week around NAPLAN.

## A sample weekday (so it is concrete)

**4:00** Snack.  
**4:10** “I spy with my first sound /sh/.” Then read *Pip and Tim* (or SPELD SA) — she points, blends. Four words on a mini whiteboard.  
**4:25** Ten-frame: “Show me 9. What do you add to make 10? Show me another way.” Four sums: 9+1, 8+2, 7+3, 10−4.  
**4:40** Monday: four animals on the table, odd one out, two different reasons.  
**4:50** Reading Eggs, timer.  
**5:00** Done. Play / outside.  
**7:15** You read *The Gruffalo* or a chapter. Two questions: “Why was she scared? What would you do?”

That is a competitive-exam foundation day. It does not look like a test.

---

## How you know it is working (Grade 1 checkpoints)

By the **end of Year 1**, you want:

- She blends a new decodable with little guessing.
- Bonds to 10 are mostly instant.
- She can give a *reason* for odd-one-out, not just point.
- She can listen to a story and say why a character did something.
- She still likes the 50 minutes. If she hates it, shorten it; a burnt child will not last to Year 8.

If decoding or bonds are still weak after a term, fix **that** before adding more reasoning games. Reasoning papers on top of shaky reading is how families waste Years 5–7.
