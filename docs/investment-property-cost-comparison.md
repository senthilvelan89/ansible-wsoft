# Investment property cost comparison

Worked examples of **monthly out-of-pocket cost** for an investment house at $600k, $700k, $800k, $850k and $1.0M in **Melbourne, Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide**.

These are estimates to help you compare cities and price bands. They are **not** financial, tax or credit advice. Actual rent, rates, stamp duty and loan pricing depend on the suburb, the contract and your lender.

---

## How to use this file

1. Read **[What each row means](#what-each-row-means)** once.
2. Open the **city table** you care about.
3. Use **Cash at settlement** for money needed on buy day.
4. Use **Cash shortfall P&I** for money you must find **every month**.
5. Use **After-tax P&I (actual OOP)** for the true yearly cost **after** the tax refund.

The tax refund is **not a monthly bill**. It is shown monthly only so it can be compared with the other monthly numbers.

## What each row means

### Loan (90%)

Amount borrowed: **90% × purchase price**.

Example: $800,000 house → loan **$720,000**. Stamp duty is **not** included in the loan.

---

### Weekly rent / yield

**Weekly rent** is a typical tenant rent for a house at that price in that city (growth-corridor / typical investor stock, not inner prestige).

**Gross yield** = (weekly rent × 52) ÷ purchase price.

Example: $590 × 52 ÷ $800,000 = **3.8%**.

Yield is a headline only. It ignores the loan, vacancy, manager, rates and tax.

---

### 10% deposit

Cash paid toward the purchase price: **10% × purchase price**.

Example: $800,000 → **$80,000**. The other 90% is the loan.

---

### Stamp duty + fees

One-off costs **on top of** the deposit, paid at or before settlement.

- **Stamp duty** (transfer duty) — state tax on the purchase. Highest in Victoria for investors; lowest in Queensland of these four cities.
- **Other buying fees** — conveyancing, building and pest inspection, loan setup, transfer and mortgage registration. About **$3,800–$4,500**.

Stamp duty is **not** a monthly cost and is **not** an annual tax deduction. It is added to the property’s cost base for capital gains tax when you sell.

---

### Cash at settlement

All cash needed on buy day:

**10% deposit + stamp duty + buying fees**

This is “how much savings I need to complete,” not the monthly bill.

Example (Melbourne $800k): $80,000 + $47,270 = **$127,270**.

---

### Cash shortfall P&I

**Money you put in each month** because rent does not cover the full bill.

**P&I** = principal and interest. The bank repayment includes interest **and** a slice that pays down the loan.

```
Cash shortfall P&I = (loan repayment + landlord expenses) − rent collected
```

**Worked example — Melbourne $800k**

Cash out each month (approx.):

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Bank loan (P&I on $720k @ 6.5%) | $4,551 |
| Property manager | ~$249 |
| Council + water | ~$275 |
| Insurance | ~$250 |
| Maintenance allowance | ~$333 |
| Land tax (spread monthly) | ~$138 |
| **Total cash out** | **~$5,796** |

Cash in: rent collected (after 2 vacant weeks) **~$2,458**.

**Shortfall ≈ $5,796 − $2,458 = $3,338.**

That $3,338 must be in the account on repayment day. The tenant does not cover it.

This is **not**:

- a tax figure (the ATO ignores principal repayments)
- profit or loss for the tax return
- the deposit or stamp duty (those are paid once)

---

### Tax refund (joint 39%) — not a cost

This is **money back**, not money you pay.

The property makes a **taxable loss** (negative gearing). That loss reduces taxable income, so you pay less income tax. The saving is the tax refund.

**Shown as a monthly number only for comparison.** The real refund is **yearly** (tax return), unless you lodge a PAYG withholding variation so less tax is taken from salary during the year.

In the Melbourne $800k example, **$1,162/month** means about **$13,900 per year**.

**What the ATO counts (not the same as cash):**

Deductible:

- Interest on the loan
- Manager, letting fee, council, water, insurance, maintenance, land tax
- Depreciation (paper deduction — you do not pay this in cash)

**Not** deductible:

- The **principal** part of the P&I repayment
- Stamp duty and deposit

**Why 39%:** property owned 50/50, incomes $150k / $150k. Each is in the 37% bracket + 2% Medicare = **39%**. Refund ≈ 39% × taxable rental loss.

**During the year you still pay the cash shortfall in full.** You do not receive the refund each month unless PAYG is varied.

---

### After-tax P&I (actual OOP)

**OOP** = out of pocket. This is the true cost **after deducting the tax refund**.

```
After-tax P&I = cash shortfall P&I − tax refund
```

Example (Melbourne $800k, joint 39%):

| | Monthly |
|---|---:|
| Cash you pay (cash shortfall P&I) | $3,338 |
| Tax refund (joint 39%) | − $1,162 |
| **After-tax P&I (actual OOP)** | **$2,176** |

During the year you still transfer **$3,338** every month. Over a full year, after the refund, the cost averages **$2,176/month**.

The refund never wipes the whole shortfall, because part of what you pay the bank is **principal**, which is not tax-deductible.

---

### After-tax interest-only

Same property, but the loan is **interest-only** (you do not pay principal yet). The bank bill is smaller, so after-tax cost is smaller.

You save cash now, but the **loan balance does not fall**. When the IO period ends, repayments jump to P&I.

---

### After-tax P&I if sole owner at 47%

Same house and same cash shortfall, **different owner and tax rate**.

- **Sole owner** = one person’s name on the title (100%).
- **47%** = that person earns **over $190,000**, so extra income is taxed at 45% + 2% Medicare.

The same taxable loss saves more tax, so after-tax OOP is lower than the joint 39% row.

This applies only if the **high earner** owns the property. If you own 50/50 at $150k each, use the **joint 39%** row. If the property is only in the **lower** income name, the refund is smaller than 39%.

## How to read the comparison

- **Perth** is the cheapest to **hold** (highest rent).
- **Brisbane** is the cheapest to **buy** (lowest stamp duty) and second-cheapest to hold.
- **Adelaide** sits in the middle.
- **Melbourne** costs the most on both fronts: highest duty, lowest yield, plus land tax every year.

None of these examples are positively geared at 6.5% on a 90% P&I loan. Closest is a **$600k Perth** house: about **$1,850/month cash** before tax, **$1,195 after tax** (joint 39%), or about **$701 after tax** on interest-only.

You still need the **pre-tax cash shortfall** in the account every month. The refund is not cash until the tax return unless you vary PAYG.

## Things that would change the numbers

- **Staff loan rate different from 6.5%** — a 0.5% move on an $800k-range 90% loan is roughly **$200–$250/month**.
- **Actual suburb rent** — $50/week extra rent is about **$180/month** after the manager’s cut.
- **Income split** — putting the property in the lower earner’s name shrinks the refund; putting it solely in a 47% earner’s name increases it.
- **Units** — add strata levies (often $3,000–$8,000+ per year) and usually drop these OOP figures unfavourably, even with higher yield.
- **Vacant month or a big repair** — a vacant month or an $8k roof year wipes several months of the “average” maintenance allowance.

Figures use 2026-style stamp duty schedules, 2026–27 resident tax rates (including 2% Medicare), and typical 2026 capital-city house rents. Confirm duty with the state revenue office calculator and rent with local listings before you buy.
